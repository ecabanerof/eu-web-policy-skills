# Website privacy and GDPR transparency

Baseline checked on 9 November 2026.
Recheck the applicable law and current regulator guidance before delivering a new review.

## Apply the correct notice

- Start with the [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng/) and [EDPB-endorsed transparency guidelines](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/article-29-working-party-guidelines-on-transparency-under-regulation-2016679_en).
  Check Article 3 for territorial scope and Articles 12–14 for accessibility, language, content and timing.
  When the controller obtains data directly, provide Article 13 information at collection.
  For indirect collection, check Article 14 timing, sources, categories, and any documented exception.
  A change to the purposes can trigger fresh information before further processing.
- Include the controller's identity and contact details and, when applicable, the representative and DPO contacts.
  List purposes and their specific legal bases, legitimate interests when relied on, recipients or their categories, retention periods or usable criteria, and rights and complaint information.
  Where relevant, add required versus optional data, consequences of withholding it, and disclosures for automated decision-making under Articles 13–14 and 22.
- Distinguish pre-contract steps requested by a person, contract necessity, legitimate interests, legal obligations, and consent.
  Record the reason and, for legal obligations, the relevant law.
  A contact form, marketing list, analytics tag, and security log may require separate analysis.
  Check Articles 8–10 and local rules for children or specially protected data when relevant.
  Avoid substituting a generic legitimate-interest claim for consent required by national terminal-access law.
- For promotional email, texts or similar direct marketing, verify [ePrivacy Article 13](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02002L0058-20091219) and the national opt-in, existing-customer exception and opt-out rules separately from the GDPR legal basis.
  Distinguish marketing from transactional messages.
  For Spain see [LSSI Article 21](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2002-13758#a21).
  For Germany see [UWG §7](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html).
- State how to exercise the rights that apply, how to withdraw consent where relied on, and the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority.
  Under Article 77, that includes in particular the Member State of habitual residence, place of work, or alleged infringement.
  Verify any named authority using the [EDPB member directory](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/about-edpb/about-edpb/members_en).
  Do not condition the right to complain on first contacting the controller.
- Check actual recipient roles, entities and access locations, including support and onward access.
  If there is a Chapter V transfer, identify the verified mechanism and applicable information about safeguards and how to obtain or access them.
  Check the Commission's [adequacy decisions](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/adequacy-decisions_en) and [SCC Decision 2021/914](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2021/914/oj).
  Verify the recipient and coverage before citing an adequacy mechanism, and check the applicable safeguards and assessment where relevant.
- Use real retention settings, legal retention duties, deletion and backup behavior, and specific criteria when an exact period varies.
  Link privacy wording with actual form, opt-in, rights-request and vendor workflows.
  A policy statement alone does not verify the processing or prove compliance.

## National starting points

- **Spain.** Verify the [LOPDGDD](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2018-16673), including Article 11 on layered information and Article 7 where a minor's consent is material.
  Use [AEPD](https://www.aepd.es/) guidance and complaint resources as applicable.
- **Germany.** Verify the [BDSG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bdsg_2018/) and the applicable federal or state authority.
  Check Section 38 for private-sector DPO requirements when the facts trigger it.
  Where Berlin is relevant, check the [Berlin authority's complaint guidance](https://www.datenschutz-berlin.de/buergerinnen-und-buerger/rechte/beschwerderecht/).
- **Other markets.** Find current national privacy and sector rules and the relevant authority.
  Escalate unresolved questions about controller identity, legal bases, children, sensitive data, tracking, transfer safeguards or high-risk profiling before treating the draft as ready for approval.
