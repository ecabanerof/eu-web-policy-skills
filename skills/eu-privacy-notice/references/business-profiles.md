# Business profiles for privacy notices

Select the profiles that match the website's actual processing and use the [privacy template](../assets/privacy-template.md).
Combine overlapping profiles by purpose and keep the evidence and missing facts in the processing map.
Check applicable laws and authorities in [legal-basis.md](legal-basis.md).
Where applicable, distinguish accepting terms, receiving a notice, and giving consent for optional processing.

## SaaS

**Ask about** sign-up, workspace accounts, billing, support, security logs, usage analytics, integrations, and information uploaded or managed by business customers.
Ask which entity sets each purpose, which services receive data, and what happens to active data and backups after an account closes.

**Draft** separate rows for the operator's own account, billing and operational activities and for customer-managed workspace data where the roles differ.
Map the role for each purpose using [EDPB controller and processor guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-072020-on-the-concepts-of-controller-and-processor-in-the-gdpr_en) where the GDPR applies.
Verify customer instructions, subprocessors, retention and transfers for managed data.
Flag any required processing agreement as a separate deliverable and direct rights requests to the verified channel for each role.

Example for a verified account flow.

> We use your name, work email address and workspace role to create your account and manage access.
> {{Verified legal grounds, recipients and retention for this purpose}}.

## AI services

**Ask about** prompts, uploaded files, chat history, outputs and feedback, and identify what the model provider and any connected tools actually receive.
Confirm the role, hosting and access locations, retention and deletion for each processing route and plan.
Check whether staff or vendors can review inputs and whether any route uses them to improve, evaluate, train or fine-tune models.

**Draft** separate purposes for delivering responses, keeping history, safeguarding the service, and model improvement or training where those activities actually exist.
For each purpose verify the legal grounds, recipient and role, transfers, retention and available controls.
Confirm statements about training and deletion against the product settings, vendor arrangements and data paths, including plan differences.
Where relevant, verify whether profiling or automated decisions trigger specific disclosures and rights.
Check any additional rules that apply to sensitive data or children's data in inputs, inferences or outputs.
For EU GDPR model development and deployment questions, use [EDPB Opinion 28/2024](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/opinion-of-the-board-art-64/opinion-282024-on-certain-data-protection-aspects-related-to_en) and check the facts of the processing.

Example for a verified model request.

> When you use {{feature}}, we process {{confirmed input categories}} to generate your response.
> {{Confirmed provider or recipient category}} receives {{data actually sent}} for {{verified purpose}}.

## E-commerce

**Ask about** checkout fields, guest or account purchases, payment and fraud services, delivery and returns, support, abandoned carts and marketing.
Identify the operator and the role of the seller, platform, payment provider and carrier for the relevant flow.

**Draft** separate purposes for taking and fulfilling orders, processing payments, keeping required records, preventing fraud, handling returns and sending promotions where applicable.
Verify which fields each recipient receives and what data the operator itself can access, including card or transaction details.
Distinguish transactional messages from promotional mail and verify any audience or advertising disclosures.
Map retention by purpose and put a short notice at collection where required.

Example for verified delivery processing.

> We use the delivery details you provide to fulfil your order and send the necessary address information to {{carrier or carrier category}}.
