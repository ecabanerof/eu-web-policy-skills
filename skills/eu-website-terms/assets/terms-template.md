# Website terms drafting template

Use the sample copy as a starting point in the reader's language.
Double braces mark values to replace with confirmed details.
Keep a section only when the site offers the relevant feature or the applicable rules require the disclosure.
Where a material value is unknown, list the gap outside the draft.

## Website operator

This website is operated by {{legal operator name}}.
You can reach us at {{appropriate contact channels}}.

Add the operator's geographic address, registry and tax details, or other disclosures where required.
Identify the contracting party separately if it differs from the site operator.

## Using the website

This website lets you {{describe the features available to visitors}}.
You may use it to {{describe the permitted uses and any permissions for materials}}.

Explain specific permissions or limits that matter to this site.
For an informational site, keep this section brief and omit the sales modules.
Check the underlying rights before claiming ownership or granting permission to reuse content.

## Inquiries and bookings

Use this module when the site offers inquiries, quotes, appointments, or event registration.
Explain when the user makes a request and when a binding booking or contract is formed.
State any real confirmation steps, charges, cancellation conditions, and seller role.

When an independent party handles a transaction, verify who sells and who performs each role.
Describe the operator's own role, including any support or obligations it retains.
Check where the required trader information is displayed before the order.
Keep the transactional section pending if the seller, allocation of roles, or required disclosures are unresolved.

## Orders and payment

For transactions the operator offers, explain what the buyer receives, the total price and additional charges, how and when payment is due, and how fulfillment works.
For recurring plans, also give the billing interval, duration, renewal, trial conversion, and cancellation steps.
Use the actual purchase and confirmation flow as evidence.
Distinguish mandatory information shown before an order from what the terms or confirmation supply.

## Withdrawal, cancellation and problems with a purchase

Use the relevant sections only after identifying the buyer type, product and national rules.
Explain any applicable statutory withdrawal period and start event, the way to exercise the right, and verified refund or return arrangements.
Use the official model withdrawal form for the market where required.
Provide an online withdrawal path only where it exists and check whether it is legally required.
Explain subscription cancellation separately, including when access and billing actually end.
Describe applicable conformity remedies and how to report a problem without limiting statutory rights.

## Changes and complaints

For an ongoing contract, explain which changes can be made, how users will be informed, and their applicable options.
Give the actual complaint channel and any required dispute resolution information.
If a governing-law clause is needed, account for mandatory consumer rights and available courts under the relevant rules.

> Nothing in these terms limits the mandatory rights that apply to you as a consumer.

Include this sentence only where a consumer module applies and review the surrounding clauses for consistency.
