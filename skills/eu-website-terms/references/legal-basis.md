# Website terms and consumer contracts

Baseline checked on 9 November 2026.
For a new draft, open the current official texts and verify which version applies to the planned launch or contract date.
Distinguish regulations, transposed directives, court decisions, and regulator guidance.

## Choose the applicable rules

| Question | Primary sources and practical checks |
| --- | --- |
| Who must be identified on an economic website? | [E-Commerce Directive 2000/31/EC, Articles 5, 10 and 11](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2000/31/oj). Verify national operator disclosures and online contracting rules, including provider name, geographic address, accessible direct contact, and registry or tax details where applicable. |
| Does the site conclude distance contracts with consumers? | [Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02011L0083-20260927), especially Articles 3, 6, 6a, 8 and 9–16 and Annex I. Verify scope, product, seller and applicable national implementation. The offer, order flow, and durable-medium confirmation may carry duties that a terms page alone cannot fulfill. |
| Can the contract exclude liability, require arbitration, or be changed by posting? | [Unfair Contract Terms Directive 93/13/EEC, Articles 3–6 and Annex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1993/13/oj). Review substantive fairness and transparency under national law, especially for unnegotiated consumer terms. |
| What protections apply to goods and digital offerings? | [Sale of Goods Directive 2019/771](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/771/oj) and [Digital Content and Digital Services Directive 2019/770](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/770/oj). Check national conformity periods, remedies, updates, and the scope for digital offerings supplied against personal data. |
| Which country's law and courts apply? | [Rome I, Article 6](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2008/593/oj) and [Brussels I bis, Articles 17–19](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2012/1215/oj). Check directed activities and exceptions. Assess choice of law and jurisdiction separately. Preserve mandatory protections and eligible consumer forums. |
| What dispute channel should be described? | [Regulation 2024/3228](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/3228/oj) discontinued the EU ODR platform and repealed the old regulation from 20 July 2025. Verify current national ADR disclosure duties and participation before naming a body. |
| Is the operator actually an intermediary or marketplace? | [Digital Services Act 2022/2065](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj) and Consumer Rights Directive Article 6a. Check the intermediary role and specific exemptions before adding terms, moderation, or trader-disclosure duties. |

## Implementation checks for online B2C

- Verify the trader, total cost including taxes and charges, payment interval, renewal, cancellation, fulfillment and complaint handling before the consumer commits.
- Check that the checkout control explicitly signals an obligation to pay where Article 8(2) applies, and that applicable information is confirmed on a durable medium.
- Where there is a withdrawal right, verify the usual 14-day period against the applicable start event, sector or market variations, and consequences of missing disclosures.
- Use the applicable model form and procedure.
  Early performance of a service, loss of withdrawal on fully performed services, and immediate delivery of digital content have distinct requirements.
  Verify any consent, acknowledgment and durable-medium confirmation required for the specific exception.
  Check the actual contract before applying an exception to a dated leisure service or personalized goods.
- [Directive 2023/2673, Articles 1 and 2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2673/oj) inserts Article 11a into the Consumer Rights Directive, with national measures scheduled to apply from 19 June 2026.
  The online withdrawal function can concern non-financial distance contracts too.
  Verify [notified transposition measures](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/NIM/?uri=CELEX:32023L2673), the national text in force, scope, and any exceptions.
  Where applicable, verify ongoing access during the withdrawal period, the initiation and confirmation controls, and the acknowledgment on a durable medium.
  Keep statutory withdrawal distinct from cancellation of ongoing renewals.

## National starting points

- **Spain.** Check [LSSI, Articles 10 and 27–28](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2002-13758) and [RDL 1/2007](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2007-20555), especially Articles 80–91 and 97–108 for relevant consumer terms and distance contracts.
  Check current official Spanish transposition and its effective date for the online withdrawal function before treating national implementation as settled.
  Verify the applicable disclosure and complaint duties under [Law 7/2017, Article 40](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2017-12659).
- **Germany.** Start with [DDG §5](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ddg/__5.html) for provider disclosures, the [BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/) for contract, withdrawal and cancellation rules, and [EGBGB Article 246a](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgbeg/art_246a__1.html) for distance selling information.
  Check the [BGB §356a electronic withdrawal function](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__356a.html) and, separately where relevant, the [BGB §312k cancellation button](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312k.html).
- **Other markets.** Find the current national legislation through the national gazette, the EU transposition record, and the consumer authority.
  Confirm territorial and sector applicability before adopting a national clause.
